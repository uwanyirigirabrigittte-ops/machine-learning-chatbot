Heading: An excellent accessory
This is an excellent accessory for the already-versatile rotary tool. The trick to its use is to recognize the inherent limitation of all rotary tools.They are high-speed, low-torque tools. The work is done in the same way that a dentist's drill works: Very light pressure lets the bit in effect shave away the material. As the manual says, you need to adjust the tool so the bit is very close to the workpiece, then slowly lower the bit into it.This means you can't use the Dremel drill press the same way you would use a full-size drill press, where you can bear down on the lever and force the bit to dig into the work. If you try to use it that way, you'll get the "slop" that some other reviewers mention.When it's used correctly-- and the manual makes it very clear how to do so-- this is a very good accessory to have.

Rating: 4.0/5.0 | Date: 09 1, 2013 | Helpful votes: 8
Heading: Are your expectations reasonable?
Elle wrote a popular one star review titled "Wish I'd Never Bought It".In that review, she expresses any number of perfectly valid issues that a buyer may have with this unit. What she doesn't address is just what constitutes 'reasonable expectations' for a product like this. If you want micro drill press capable of high RPM and .001 tolerances, did you REALLY think you were going to get that from a $40 Dremel add-on?Really?If you want a microdrill capable of beating ISO standards for medical machining, they're definitely out there but you ain't gunna get it for $40. Also, I won't make any assumptions about your experience with tools like this but if ever the old saying "Let the tool do the work" applies, it applies to this. If you're working with metal, you need to drill S-L-O-W-L-Y down. Its not hard to envision someone not understanding this, trying to drill on a metal workpiece, wrenching on the drill arm and wondering why its not consistent. Like all mini tools (for example, Taig lathes, etc) there will always be a small group of people who come in with totally unreasonable expectations and leave disgruntled.So, what about for the rest of us who DON'T try to row the ocean with a soup ladle and are buying this machine in the context of ordinary utility? For me, it gets 4 stars.Firstly, the 'workstation' moniker makes dandy sense from a marketing perspective- it kinda fluffs up the truth of the matter, makes it seems like you're getting a highly dynamic piece of tooling. Really, this is precisely what it looks like. A drill press attachment for a Dremel.I'd even venture a wager that a lot of people buy this without realizing that for only a few dollars more, they can purchase an imported benchtop drill press that is likely FAR more suited for their needs than this will be.This is for gadget-a-holics or people who have a genuine need for high RPM micro drilling.The catch-22 for the latter- those in need of high RPM microdrilling- is the very conundrum expressed in Elle's review. If you need high rpms to make tiny little holes, you very well may need a degree of precision that this cannot offer out of the box.But what about once it's already out of the box? Can it be improved?The answer is yes. You need to tighten bolts, increase bearing surfaces here and there... Most importantly, the Dremel itself is threaded into a 'cup' that leaves a ton of gap between the Dremel body and the workstation mounting point. It should be a mated fit but for some stupid reason, it's just flopping around in there. This is a dumb design flaw. It is something you're going to have to remedy if you want super precise and repeatable cuts since the plastic threads on the Dremel body and the plastic nut that holds it into the unit just isn't cut out for maintaining high accuracy. It can be fixed.

Rating: 3.0/5.0 | Date: 04 7, 2013 | Helpful votes: 2
Heading: Not quite a serious tool ... but could be
Initial impressions on unboxing were of a decent quality product. Materials are nice and the parts go together well. Upon setting it up, it's clear that the vertical position is off (relative to the baseplate) by 1 - 2 degrees, as others have mentioned. In terms of a DIY fix, it might help to extend the curved slot that limits the rotary movement; I have not yet fully investigated. This may not be too much of a problem when drilling thin sheet but it certainly does detract from the perceived quality.When I tried some test holes in plywood with a 3/64" bit the tool torqued sideways as soon as the bit touched the work, flexing the thin drill bit alarmingly. The main problem here seems to be poor design of the set screws that go through the left side of the body and into the machined groove in the solid rod. Bafflingly, the set screws are tapered so that they have no chance of contacting the sides of the groove. I made bushings from sections of a ballpoint pen refill and this improved things considerably. I am sure you could still get the thing to flex, but it no longer wobbles if the breeze catches it. It would be so easy for Dremel to fix this major achilles heel that you have to wonder why they don't. There is still a (much smaller) residual side-to-side slop arising from a slight gap between the solid rod and the blue plastic guide.This is basically a good product. If these very simple issues were fixed it would be worth 5 stars. I feel it's around 3.5 now so am giving it three to try to drag the average in that direction.UPDATE: I have made the vertical position vertical by extending the curved slot by 2-3mm. To separate the blue pieces I had to remove the outer grey trim (3 screws), under which is a side cover held by another 3 screws. Take this off and you'll be able to loosen the centre screw with a flat screwdriver. The blue plastic is not as tough as it looks and it was easy to remove material with a 45degree blade in an X-acto knife. Finally my drill bit is vertical, as it should have been in the first place!

Heading: Useful accessory, but low accuracy compared to a real drill press
This is a nice accessory with lots of potential uses, but don't expect tons of accuracy when using it as a drill press.As other reviewers have mentioned, when you first take this unit out of the box it will likely be very poorly calibrated with lots of play at the tip of the bit. After spending some time to set it up properly you can get it reasonably accurate but in my case there was still some play in the press mechanism and excessive runout at the spindle/collet which I was unable to eliminate.I spent way too much time fiddling with this thing trying to get it to drill small holes with precision before I bit the bullet and bought a real drill press. After setting up the real drill press the difference in accuracy was immediately evident and I was able to complete my project very quickly. So if you need to drill small and very precisely spaced holes you should probably get a real drill press as this unit will likely only frustrate you. For small projects that don't require a ton of precision this is an acceptable substitute for a drill press.Aside from drilling, this workstation opens up a lot of other uses for the dremel, I have found it to be very handy for sanding bevels at a controlled angle and making angled cuts in small workpieces.
