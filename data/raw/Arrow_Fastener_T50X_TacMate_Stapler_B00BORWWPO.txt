Heading: Heavy Duty (Loads of Penetrating Power), Lightweight, Easy Loading, BUT (1) Doesn't Work With Brads And (2) Doesn't Tack Wires
This staple gun has a lot of penetration power. It's more powerful than the stapler I picked up from Ace Hardware a few years ago. It will fully penetrate softwood, hardwood, plywood, plastic sheets, and other materials of comparable thickness/hardness. I tested this staple gun on pine (a softwood) and birch (a hardwood) and the staples were able to punch through completely.BUT unlike my Ace Hardware staple gun (1) this staple gun won't accept brad nails and (2) it cannot be used for tacking wire (it lacks a mechanism for controlling the depth of penetration).For a dual purpose staple gun that accepts both staples and brad nails, consider theArrow T50PBN Pro Staple and Nail Gun. For tacking wires, Arrow makes a staple gun for just that -- a dedicated wiring tacker -- theArrow Fastener T59 Wiring Tacker.For the ultimate all-in-one staple gun that accepts BOTH staples and brad nails, AND functions as a wire tacker, consider theStanley TR250 SharpShooter Plus Heavy-Duty Staple/Brad Nail Gun.---I'm disappointed that this staple gun lacks the mechanism for controlling the depth of penetration (for use in tacking wires). At the current price (~24), it's not unreasonable for Arrow to have included this feature as well. The Verdict: If you need a dedicated staple gun (i.e. one for stapling ONLY), this one might be an deal choice since it's heavy duty yet lightweight. However, if you're looking for staple gun that does staples and brads, and functions as a wire tacker, you will do well to look elsewhere.

Heading: Good design, easy to use, and comes with staples! Minor safety hazard.
Well everyone else already said it was easy to use...but the reason everyone is saying that is because it's true!The staples are very easy to put in (though it helps to reach the instructions to understand since it's not exactly like your standard office stapler...still you could probably figure it out on your own).And the design is great. It's very easy to press up against whatever it is you want to use it on and push down on the handle. The force is enough that it's not just going to spitting staples like crazy, but that it's easy to press down without a lot of hand strength. Comfortable to hold for a period of time and keep using. Does drive in the staples fast enough to be effective on jobs like upholstery.One minor concern I have is safety which is why there is 4 stars - even though it's not a hair trigger I do see how someone could drop it or pick it up unthinkingly and shoot out a staple. Or, even worse, a child who doesn't fully understand it and respect it could misuse it or accidentally cause injury. The staple does come out with enough force to hurt depending on where it is in relation to skin, eyes, breakable objects, etc. It would be nice if there was something to inhibit that - I little safety latch or something that prevented it from fully pushing down. Because of this, I will be storing mine unloaded.Another nice thing about it - it does come with a box of 8 mm stables (1250 of them), so unless you need a different size, this will be ready to use as soon as you receive it. If you're like me and only plan to use this for small jobs (re-tacking upholstery or the like) it may be quite a while before you need to buy more staples.

Heading: Easy to use
This is staple gun for home projects. It has a metal frame that is mostly encased in rubber to make it easy to hold. It accepts staples in 4 sizes: 1/4&#34; (6 mm), 5/16&#34; (8 mm), 3/8 (10 mm), 1/2 (12 mm). You first press the blue lock release at the back of the stapler to release the slider, then drop the staples into the reservoir upside down. Push the slider back in until the blue button locks again, and you're ready to go. There are pictures on the side of the slider that try to explain this symbolically, but I couldn't make heads or tails of the pictures. In any case, once the stapler was loaded, I found it quite easy to use. I had no trouble driving staples into hard wood surfaces, even though I have small hands and not a lot of hand strength. I also liked the fact that there was no recoil. Some other staple guns that I have used really make your hand sting, but this one is quite comfortable. This is a great tool for home projects, or for hanging posters around town.

Rating: 4.0/5.0 | Date: 11 20, 2013 | Helpful votes: 0
Heading: Lightweight, easy to load, easy to use, long-term use may be an issue
The staple gun is one of those things that we use once or twice a year - especially during haunted house season. It's great for stapling plastic to wood to create the frame of the haunt...but if you're not building a haunt you can still get use out of it :-).Loading the staples is quick and easy and this package comes with a box so you can get started right away. The handle is small and made of a plastic material that is smooth - no rough edges on ours which can sometimes happen on machined plastic. It is advertised as fitting small to medium hands, and that it does.Depressing the handle is easy to do, but this is where it differs from other (better) staple guns. On this device you're depressing the handle away from the side that does the actual stapling. This means the weight you're putting on it is at the back and you've got to push forward to hold it against the wood (or whatever you're stapling into). We've had MUCH better success with staple guns where the handle is reversed and you're putting pressure directly into the side that sends the staple downward.Although that might seem counter-intuitive, if you're doing a LOT of staple gunning you're going to notice a difference in how your wrist feels and the level of fatigue you experience. And that's also where the long-term use may become an issue with this staple gun. After a round of stapling it could really be felt in the wrist and arm - due to the &#34;pushing forward&#34; required to hold it against the wood.And so, for light jobs where you aren't spending several hours stapling things down this device works just fine and gets a solid 4 stars. For jobs that require consistent and long-term stapling I'm apt to grab my &#34;reversed&#34; staple gun and use that one, because it works better for my wrist and where the pressure has to be applied.
