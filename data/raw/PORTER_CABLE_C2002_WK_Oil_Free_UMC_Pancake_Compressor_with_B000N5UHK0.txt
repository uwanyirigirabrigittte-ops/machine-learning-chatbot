--- REVIEW 3 ---
Rating: 4.0/5.0 | Date: 01 12, 2012 | Helpful votes: 6
Heading: Great Little Compressor
Don't be too put off by the instructions -- they read like a prescription drug disclaimer -- there's very little about how to use the compressor -- they're all about how not to use it. Instructions notwithstanding, the compressor worked as advertised coming to a tank pressure of 150 psi within a minute, or so (after I closed the drain valve, which arrived open). It's certainly not "whisper" quiet but it's not deafening either. The gauges can become slightly misaligned but it's no big deal straightening them. The regulator works well and allows you to avoid overinflating/bursting items (like skinny bike tires). The tire chuck provided with the compressor is so "stubby" that's it's a bit hard to use -- I ordered (from Amazon) a Milton S693 chuck for $12 and it solved my problem with the stubby chuck. The $159 price is outweighed by the convenience of not having to drop a bunch of quarters into the local gas station's compressor and then rushing around hoping that you can get to all four tires before the compressor times-out. I'm sure I'll find a lot of things to do with the Porter-Cable unit other than just inflating car tires. I must admit that I applied $150 of gift cards to the purchase so I only paid $9 -- I might have been a little reluctant to spend the whole $159 just for the convenience.

Rating: 4.0/5.0 | Date: 05 23, 2012 | Helpful votes: 3
Heading: Super lightweight compressor!
I had my first Porter Cable C2002 6 gallon pancake compressor for 4 years, or so, before it was finally stolen from my back yard.No problem. I figured it would be a good excuse to buy a better one. Did some research, again. And 4 years later, I still can't find a better compressor for my needs.Good: It holds more air, at a higher pressure, and produces a higher CFM than anything else its weight. Period. And it's cheap.Bad: It's pretty loud. And my first developed a leak after 6 months of relatively light use. This leak was coming from the compressor/motor. Took it to a service center and it was fixed in a week or so.After that, the only issue I had was, like another reviewer noted, a leaking drain valve. This, however, it no big deal, at all.1. If you use it properly, it probably won't leak, anyway. The reason I messed mine up is I took to draining the tank when fully pressurized. This is hard on the O-ring. If you bleed the tank down to 20-30 PSI before you crack the vale, and you leave at least 20-30 PSI in it when not in use, it should last a long time.2. Even if you lose the seal on this vale, fer gosh sake, you don't have to return it and get another unit. Unscrew the valve at the base. Take out the O-ring. Bring it to the hardware store and find a replacement. If you're feeling saucy, splurge the 50 cents and buy 2-3 spares.
