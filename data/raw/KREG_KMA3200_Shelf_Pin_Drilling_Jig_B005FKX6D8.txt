Rating: 2.0/5.0 | Date: 12 5, 2012 | Helpful votes: 50
Heading: Not cool....
I just purchased a Kreg KMA3200 shelving jig.The drill bit included in my kit, although appearing to be sharp, is doing a terrible job drilling in oak ply. The edges of the holes are raised and the veneer is often being torn, with shards remaining attached to the edges of the hole. It won't be possible to sand the holes smooth without going through the veneer.I switched to a cheap spur bit (with slightly longer spurs) and the results are what I expected...cleanly cut with virtually no surface disturbance..BTW, the jig itself works well. The steel sleeves in the guide holes are long enough to keep the drill bit straight and the jig is easy to align and move up the board. Alone as a jig, I'd give it four stars. Add that bit in and it drops to two.Update: Kreg's support department responded quickly to my email and sent me a new drill bit. It's different than the one that was in the kit and works much better. If you buy this kit and your bit is made from two pieces of metal - a black oxide hex base with a shiny drill inset - be careful. If it's machined from one piece of metal, it's likely the same as the replacement.

--- REVIEW 2 ---
Rating: 5.0/5.0 | Date: 12 15, 2011 | Helpful votes: 28
Heading: I always wanted a shelf pin jig but they were too expensive until now
Most of the shelf pin jigs were more money than I wanted to spend for an operation that I rarely use. They were also too big taking up too much valuable space. If you drill shelf pin holes all of the time they are well worth it though, and you will get perfect registration each time without having to remove and reinsert this jig multiple times. You could even use this Kreg to make up a temporary full size jig out of a three inch wide piece of stock and then throw it away.I am not dissuading others from using this as a perfectly good stand alone jig for creating finish pin projects. This will do the job all by itself. They give you a drill block with 6 pin hole metal sheathed guides. The idea is to use a reference pin in the last hole, after moving the jig up the line. They also give you an edge guide piece that you can snap into either side to you have the option of creating a 1" or 2" offset from the edge for your pins. You simply flip the jig and place the edge guide on the proper side.They also give you an end bracket in the event you wanted to daisy chain these thereby making a longer unit, one that you wouldn't have to reposition, and reclamp so many times. That is a cool feature because with this unit being so inexpensive, you could connect as many as you want to give you the ideal length, and make storage of the overall unit something you can stack up in a very small box.They also provide a brad point bit with an adjustable collar secured by an Allen bolt so you can adjust the depth of the hole. They give you a depth gauge on the back that is actually two troughs that the bit falls into, and a securing lever so it doesn't fall out while you are securing the collar.Since drilling shelf pins is something you want to do accurately, but not something that requires .001 tolerances, utilizing this jig with multiple positions as you moves up a work piece, the accuracy of a guide pin will give you consistent results. The real bottom line is that at this price point, you can't afford not to have one. I consider it a welcome addition to the arsenal.

--- REVIEW 7 ---
Rating: 5.0/5.0 | Date: 11 22, 2011 | Helpful votes: 7
Heading: Works great - but only on 1/4" pins - so far
When making cabinets, you need a quick, easy way to drill the shelf pin holes (if you are using pins). The holes need to be straight, square, and NOT go through to the other side of the cabinet.Kreg's Shelf Pin Drilling Jig isn't the first to market, but it's a solid entry. They use a flat bottom drill bit with a stop collar, so you get a flat hole, and can determine how deep it goes. The holes are spaced at the standard 32mm apart. The jig can easily be positioned for holes located 1" or 2" from the edge of the board. The guide easily comes off, if you want to place holes elsewhere. There are six, steel lined guide holes. Since most shelves have more than six adjustments, you move the guide up, and pin the jig to an existing hole with the supplied locating pin. It's much simpler than it sounds.If you want to spend less time moving the clamp, they can be joined together - as many as you can afford.There's even storage in the unit for the drill bit and locator pin.It's easier to use than it is to describe. To show how easy it is, I tried to link to a YouTube video, but that isn't allowed.Rockler JIG IT Shelving Jig and 1/4'' Bit SetIt's similar (in function) to this item, but I would choose the Kreg for it's smaller size and the ability to drill more holes before re-positioning the jig.The only reason I gave it 4 stars is that there's no option for 5mm holes (at least there's no mention of it in the instructions).Also, it's so new, that there's no pricing yet. Kreg's prices are usually reasonable, so I assume this will be too.After final pricing and clarification on 5mm holes, I may change my rating to 5 stars.--- Edit ---Kreg says there's a 5mm bit available separately.MSRP is $34.99 - a very fair price for what is included.

--- REVIEW 16 ---
Rating: 5.0/5.0 | Date: 02 7, 2013 | Helpful votes: 3
Heading: Excellent jig
I used this jig to add more holes to two bookshelf units. Quite easy to use and the results were perfect. I used the included locating pin and a round shelf pin in another hole to precisely line up the jig for the intermediate holes. If you place the jig over an existing hole it is quite easy to set the depth collar on the drill bit so that all the new holes will be exactly the same depth as the existing ones. I ordered the 1/4 jig kit which includes a 1/4 drill bit and then ordered the Kreg 5mm shelf pin jig drill bit separately. The holes in the plastic jig itself are all 1/4 inch. The 5mm drill bit has a 1/4 inch shaft and then steps down to 5mm. You don't need two jigs - just one jig and the two Kreg drill bits. Note that a normal 5mm drill bit with a 5mm shaft will not work properly with this jig since the holes are all 1/4 inch. You need the Kreg 5mm bit for this to work properly. That said, if you want two jigs so that you can connect them into a longer jig it would make sense to order a 1/4 inch kit and a 5mm kit so that you ended up with both size bits.
