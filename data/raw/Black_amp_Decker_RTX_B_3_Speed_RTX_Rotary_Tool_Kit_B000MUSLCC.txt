Heading: Wow...Great alternative to Dremels
I still have and use my Dremel #245 and my Dremel cordless MiniMite 750, but after reading the other reviews on this black and decker I gave it a try and bought one. My Dremel 245 gets burning hot fast. Wow what a great alternative to Dremel models. My Dremel #245 would heat up tremendously after a short period of use. (Yes I checked the bushings on my Dremel 245 and they are still good on this unit). The black and decker with the 3 speeds was powerful and relatively not too noisy as well. It has a good feel to it, sure its only slightly more girth than a Dremel but the weight distribution and the rubberized plastic where you can hold it made it very comfortable in the hand. Black and Decker has a great alternative and it fits many of the Dremel accessories I have. I have yet to encounter one that didn't fit yet. I used it with my Dremel Chain Saw sharpening tool and it fits and screws on perfectly. Note the amazon description says storage case, its not a carrying case that comes with your Black and Decker, its a draw string black plastic pouch bag. The tool sort of fits in that bag. I guess to keep the cost down they opted to give you a pouch bag instead of a hard plastic carrying case, No matter. The tool itself is excellent. It has a 3 speed dial labeled L (12,000), M (24,000) , H (30,000) . Who needs more than that? I'm very happy with it. If you always had a dremel like me, give this black and decker a try and you will be surprised at the high quality of this rotary tool.Update (Jan 2013) Get the dremel keyless chuck. It fits and works on this Black and Decker Tool and you will never have to bother with the collet and collet wrench again. Really. Once you do that, you will never go back to using collets on this black and decker rotary tool.

Heading: B&D delivers a very functional, powerful, and economical rotary tool&#8230;
My battery poweredDremel 7300did a good job, except that the battery always seemed to be dead, whenever I wanted to use the tool. This provided the motivation for buying an AC powered rotary tool. The RTX-B motor has three speeds, 12,000 RPM, 24,000 RPM, and 30,000 RPM. I generally use the lowest speed for better control, but it&#8217;s great to have a lot more power available if it is needed. The battery powered Dremel 7300 has a top speed of 13,000 RPM, and delivers much less torque.The RTX-B has an ergonomic design, with a rubberized area near the front, that provides a nice grip. The tool incorporates a pretty well designed flip lock safety mechanism to lock the shaft. Replacing fittings and accessories isn&#8217;t as easy as the Dremel, but at least the chuck accepts all my old Dremel accessories, and does a superior job with them. This is fortunate, as when it comes to accessories, Dremel&#8217;s product line is far superior, leaving B&D; in the dust.Some may not think it necessary, but with a rotary tool capable of high speeds, wearing safety glasses is a must. A carrying bag that isn&#8217;t well suited to the tool is included, along with some screwdriver bits.While Dremel accessories are the best, they don&#8217;t necessarily have to be attached to a Dremel brand rotary tool. Although the price has risen to close to $30, the B&D;&#8217;s RTX-B is a solid performer, which still remains less expensive than the Dremel 200 kit, and I don&#8217;t have any second thoughts about buying one.
