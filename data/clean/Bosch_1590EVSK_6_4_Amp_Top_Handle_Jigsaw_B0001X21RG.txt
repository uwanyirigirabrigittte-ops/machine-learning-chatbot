Heading: Wow - what a nice jig saw. The Bosch 1590EVSK is smooth, powerful and easy to handle. But, with any cutting tool, half of the credit or blame belongs to the blade used. The speed control circuitry held a constant blade speed regardless of the rate I fed the tool through the stock. Of course there are limits and if pushed too fast the blade would slow down somewhat.Another reviewer mentioned heat buildup with this Bosch. I used this jig to cut four skirtboards for my staircase. It took about an hour to make all of the cuts. The Bosch was slighty warm but not hot. Perhaps working in an unfinished house at 65 degrees was part of the reason I did not observe any objectional temperatures.The Bosch is a heavier-than-most jigsaw. However, the extra weight, coupled with a large shoe size makes it easy to keep the tool firmly in contact with the material. My previous B&D; jigsaw had a small shoe and as a result was "tippy" especially when cutting near the edge of the stock. I have not observed this behavior with the Bosch. The mass also aids in dampening vibration.The blower works well at higher speeds, but if you select a low speed setting the tool does not generate enough air to clear the cut line of debris.One improvement would be the inclusion of a light source near the blade. My 40-something eyes don't focus as well as they used to and a bit of supplemental light near the blade would be welcome.At first I was hesitant to spend so much on a jigsaw. But then, my previous saw was an economy priced B&D; that I cursed every time I used it. With the Bosch I look forward to cutting with a jigsaw and have never regrettted spending the extra money for it.

--- REVIEW 8 ---
Rating: 5.0/5.0 | Date: 02 10, 2009 | Helpful votes: 9
Heading: The Magnum Holeshooter of Jigsaws
I was told you couldn't go wrong. That was an understatement.Running down the list:* Yes, very tight scroll cuts. No, you can't twist the blade violently to make a 90 degree cut. That's just common sense physics and not what people mean by "square" cuts. It does plunge cut quite nicely with the the right blade though. If the blade is wandering on you, press the big shiny red button and read the manual fer cryin' out loud. I didn't even have one and I figured out what you needed to lock down the precision control mechanism.* It couldn't be easier or more obvious how to change blades if a little gnome hopped out of a hole in the gearbox and did it for you. Seriously.* It's the right weight in my opinion which aids in control and stability but most people would get tired fast using it on walls or ceilings. Get a JS5 or dig deep for the festool if you need a lighter saw for this purpose or just get a sawzall if you don't need a clean cut.* As far as bevel control, straight and 45 degree bevel cuts are really easy to set up because there are indentations you can slide the foot back on for these settings. You can set up any angle in between but it probably takes a little trial and error to get precision out of this. The surfaces that come into contact are rough (probably to hold tighter when locked) so you basically have to loosen it enough for them to not be in contact which is kind of wobbly and clumsy. It's a minor but standout gripe in an otherwise near-perfectly engineered tool and the friction that makes a smooth adjustment tricky is probably also part of what keeps the setting rock solid when you lock it down. That is IF you know there is a lever and a dial for locking/unlocking and tightening/loosening. That's another RTFM issue some people are missing, I suspect.* I have no idea why you would need variable speed trigger control on it. It's too smooth of an operator to need a soft start and I would anticipate no trouble continuing an aborted cut. It's not going to jerk around on you when you start it up again. I think Bosch's engineers were reasonable in assuming you'd be cutting one type of material at a time with it so why would you need to adjust speed as you go?* Blower works great. The dust collection attachment looks like it will work well but I haven't had a chance to purchase/try it yet.* As for longevity/toughness: I got mine fairly heavily used. The beat-up case and marks and wood glue gunked up on the saw suggest quite a bit of use from an on-site contractor who wasn't a very light touch. Also some abuse/misuse: there are scratch marks on the foot-loosening dial that suggest he didn't RTFM either and the blade in it is pretty seriously scorched for the edge its teeth are still holding. In spite of all this, I can't imagine the saw ever worked better than it does now.* Controls: 3 orbital settings or no orbital on one switch. Blower toggle. Precision guide toggle. Foot locking lever for locking in a new bevel setting. Foot loosening/tightening dial for setting the bevel before locking the foot again. Speed control in the back where it could be a touch awkward to reach for during use but I don't see why you would need to. Nonvariable speed trigger with a rightie/leftie-friendly trigger lock that would come in handy if you want to mount it upside down to use like a scroll saw table.* Cuts that you won't have to sand if you use the right blades. You can credit the dual-edge-cutting miracle blades Bosch makes for this (I was using one of the older ones) but the negligible vibration and control the saw gives you can't hurt.I can't tell you this is the best jigsaw, not having tried its immediate competitors but I can certainly say it's a 5-star experience and much better than not going wrong. Definitely worth spending the extra bucks over some black & decker POS and the JS5 is probably better than any disposable consumer grade model if the cost is an issue for you.

Heading: good saw, minor quibbles
An all-around good saw with low vibration, good stability, and a good secure blade lock and release mechanism. Solid performance so far, although it does come with a surprisingly short (1 year) warranty.The only minor quibbles:1. The "precision cut" blade clamp is a design that appears to have left engineering out of the loop. Two metal jaws in constant contact with a metal blade moving at 3000 spm, with no lubrication or rollers. I initially thought the saw had a bad motor bearing as it consistently squeaked to a stop. Turned out to be the blade squeaking against these clamp jaws. And no, I did not even engage the precision cut clamp button. Many of the blade styles are wide & thick enough that there is constant contact with the clamp jaws, even when they are "open."2. The case. Easily the cheapest, shoddiest case I've seen wrapped around a power tool in years.But overall a good tool and a good buy.
