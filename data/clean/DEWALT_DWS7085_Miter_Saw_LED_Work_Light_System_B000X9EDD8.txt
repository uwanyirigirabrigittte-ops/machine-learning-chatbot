Rating: 3.0/5.0 | Date: 01 7, 2011 | Helpful votes: 40
Heading: Unique Concept but hardly makes up for not having a laser line
After discovering my $210 Dewalt Miter saw did not have a laser line like every other Miter-saw on the market I went shopping. I soon discovered that the add on laser system made for my saw ($80) apparently didn't work well. Was never true and required constant fiddling. The only other option is this "work light" which also casts a shadow line on the wood your cutting. I personally think the engineers were surprised to see the shadow line and decided to advertise it as a feature. The first thing you should know is the shadow appears as you press the saw closer to the board. When the blade is fully up, the shadow is blurred by the teeth on the blade. You can sort of, kinda tell where it is on you project wood with the blade fully up. Push the blade downward and the shadow line becomes sharper. Its not laser line sharp but still clear enough to make a precision cut. (I had a Craftsman with laser and can tell you they can be way off. I had to compensate by placing the blade behind the line.) The work light is bright and useful as well. Installation consists of removing the top of the handle and installing the switch into two existing wire connections. You have to pay attention to which side the clip on the plugs are to get them oriented correctly. You then have to maneuver the top piece on while pressing the connectors to one side to get a flush fit. The second piece installs on the blade housing. Pretty much a no brainer.Bottom line: if you have a Dewalt Miter-saw and plan on keeping it you need this work light. It should of been on the saw when you bought it, but thats Dewalt. You will suffer laser envy when your friends break out their Mitre saws...but unfortunately the Dewalt add on laser is a disaster. So welcome to the shadows...(oh,yes you can see it in the daylight too but the saw has to be pretty far down).

--- REVIEW 4 ---
Rating: 4.0/5.0 | Date: 10 26, 2009 | Helpful votes: 13
Heading: In many respects, superior to a laser! Like the majority of other opinions written here, DeWalt's LED worklight solution is a great alternative to finicky lasers. I've extensively used two laser units, LaserKerf's add-on unit on a DeWalt 708 (quite nice) and Makita's joke of a laser on their LS1016L 10" miter.Anyhow... as has been already pointed out, the LED needs no adjustment and projects a shadow the width of the kerf. My cuts are dead on.Detractors? A few.The shadow isn't crisp and clear until the blade is lowered at least half-way to the workpiece. This is a minor inconvenience because I've taken to casting my lasers (and now shadows) directly onto a tape measure held against the work piece, eliminating the need to pencil-mark the wood. If I had three hands, this wouldn't be an issue. As I only have two, I have to set the tape measure, lower the blade, adjust my piece without jiggling the tape out of place, then remove the tape without jostling the workpiece out of place.Installation is a little cramped. The small connectors that others have noted weren't really a problem, and they're actually quite big when they have to sit just so in the tiny compartment on the saw, nested in amongst the various wires. On my DW717, it took a couple of tries of repositioning the connectors and wires to be able to get the LED unit to set flush on the saw.The switch is tiny AND covered with a loose rubber cover, so it's a bit of an inconvenience to turn the LED on and off if using the saw intermittently. It'd be great if it was a pushbutton instead of a rocker switch, and even better if it had a time-out function of some sort after, say, 15 or 30 minutes of inactivity on the saw.Lastly, for some strange reason, this LED causes a bunch of static interference on my radio whenever it's powered on.All-in-all, a great device that removes ALL of the guess work out of my cuts.

Lasers give people the false sense of security (accuracy) because lasers are precise by nature but how it is mounted will determine how accurate your cuts are. And I've had issues with the far and near side not being perfectly at a 90 degree angle to the guide rail. These issues aren't a big deal when framing, but don't attempt finishing work without double checking the old fashion way. Another small laser issue, we would always forget to turn it off because it is only noticable when you look at it. I am liking the idea of using the LED light instead of the laser because I always like to drop the blade (not spinning) on my cut line to perfectly line things up. This light will make it so i don't have to squint as much to see where the blade is going to land.

Heading: So much better than a laser
Like many others have stated, the DeWalt LED light is much better than a laser for my use. My last mitersaw had a laser and it was a pain to align and hard to see, even inside my wood shop. The light works very well, with only one small caveat, IMHO. The light (or shadow) only gets sharp to align closely to a critical (fine measurement) cut as the blade gets very close to the piece being cut. The closer you get, the sharper the shadow image becomes, so a final small alignment of your piece can only occur at that point, so clamping your piece can be a problem.However, I rarely ever need to clamp the piece I am cutting. I mostly cut 13/16" material (or less) hardwood and am using an 80-tooth blade. I cut slowly and rarely have a need to clamp, so this is not a major issue for me, but may be for others. Bottom line, so far, I like it (especially compared to a laser).

The shadow cast from the blade is not real defined until the blade approaches the work piece, unlike lasers that always throw a defined bright colored line on the work piece.

Sometimes we tend to over complicate things. As is the case with this little gem, lasers while useful & nerdy cool are annoyingly limited for making sighting tasks simple & efficient.
