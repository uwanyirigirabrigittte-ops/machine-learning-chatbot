--- REVIEW 1 ---
Rating: 5.0/5.0 | Date: 10 24, 2013 | Helpful votes: 4
Heading: Oh you crafty thing
What crafty person doesn't have a staple gun in their arsenal? I do, but the one have is heavy, clunky, and not that comfortable to use. This one is the opposite of that. It has a silicone topped handle, is lightweight, and pretty easy to load.This stapler comes with picture-instructions, that is, without words. I am pretty good at figuring things out without directions, but this one actually isn't that easy. It has a black end that you pull out, that part was easy enough. I pulled it, and this metal slide attached to the black part came out, with a top part that is black, that has a "no staples here" circle with a diagonal line through it. It actually took me about three minutes to understand where the staples were supposed to go, and that was after I tried to figure out what the picture in the instructions was telling me. You actually just lay the strip of staples (they are sort of glued together, like regular paper stapler strips are) with the points facing out, on the underside of the body of the gun. Then you slide the black part back in, and you are set to start stapling. The front of the gun has a picture of a staple and an arrow that indicates the direction of the staple. So as many other reviewers have said, the gun looks backwards when you in "ready position" for stapling. The little black end where we pulled out slide to load is at the end, think of that as the butt instead of thinking it's the front, and you won't be holding your gun backwards.This gun comes with a 1,000 box of JT21 staples, which are size #215 in standard terms. These are 5/16" or 8mm high. The gun also can staple 6mm and 10mm staples. This is a great lightweight stapler for household use.

Heading: Great around-the-house tool
This is a great little staple gun. It's not heavy-duty, but it gets the job done. Thankfully, it has the forward action, which means that you press down against the surface you want to staple instead of holding against the surface while pulling a trigger-handle away from the surface as with traditional staple guns.This one comes with a single small box of staples, but unless you're going hog wild, that will last you a good long time. It has 1000 staples, after all. Just be aware that they are short staples, so you cannot fasten anything terribly long, just a quarter inch or so. This stapler lacks the power to punch through heavier woods or concrete, but it's great on sheetrock and less dense woods.

--- REVIEW 15 ---
Rating: 3.0/5.0 | Date: 12 4, 2013 | Helpful votes: 0
Heading: Comfortable, simple to use *light* duty stapler
The grippy materials help keep your hand from slipping, and the action is triggered by the palm-rest in the opposite way I'm used to with staplers - it hinges at the rear of the stapler, unlike most I've seen where the hinge it at the front. I found it fairly easy to compress the handle enough to staple. These types of staplers alway require a bit extra energy than you might expect coming from a desktop stapler, but this one's not bad.Loading is also easy. Turn the stapler over and pull at the black triangular piece at the back of the base and it slides out. Drop staples into the cavity and close the sliding part. Done.I tested the staples on a small oak board and the staples were consistently embedded about 1/8", leaving a bit more than 1/16" of the staple exposed. This may or may not be the case with lighter woods - this is what I had on hand. It simple doesn't have the power to drive the staple all the way into this wood, so it will need to be for light-duty only (and may prove perfect for hanging Christmas lights because it leaves some of the staple exposed.)The included box of staples is handy. Unless you're stapling a lot, this should last quite a while.Packaging is pretty terrible, for those who worry about such things. It's packed in a plastic blister case, with most of the stapler exposed to allow for testing it out. The stapler is held in by a couple of heat-sealed portions of the blister case and prove a bit difficult to cut around to free the stapler. There's also a small instruction sheet included that really isn't needed once you figure out how to open the staple drawer.

Heading: Wonderful for people like me with poor hand strength
I've been using theArrow Co 5700 PowerShot Heavy Duty Staple Gunfor years and I like it so much, I've given one to each of my adult children. The unique body design is so much easier on my hands than traditional style staplers.This new light duty version by Arrow features the same unique design, but is much smaller and lighter in weight. The body is made of a sturdy plastic and the whole thing weighs under a quarter of a pound. It was so lightweight that I really didn't believe the packaging which said it is perfect for small upholstery projects like covering a chair seat. However, I covered 10 chair seats and this stapler had no problem shooting the included 5/16" staples into the wooden seat base. It also works great for cork boards and, using 3/8" staples (not included), it works just fine on drywall.The plastic clamshell that it's packaged in is a pain to open so be warned. Also, be careful not to discard the folded paper instruction sheet because you will need it. It also comes with a box of 1000 5/16" staples. (You can also use 1/4" and 3/8" staples in this stapler, but they are light duty, thin wire staples so be careful to purchase the correct ones.)The first time you load staples into this stapler, refer to the instructions. It's not like any standard stapler. You turn the stapler over and load the staples from the bottom. You also need to take note of which end the staples come out of. It's not the end you'd think at first glance. However, once you figure it out, this stapler works great. HINT: Press the stapler itself down firmly on the item you are stapling and then squeeze the handle. It takes very little pressure to fire a staple - something I really appreciate when covering 10 chair seats!I think this is a wonderful stapler for light duty projects and craft projects - especially for women or people with arthritis or diminished hand strength. I also recommend its big brother, the PowerShot Heavy Duty Stapler, for heavier use and applications.
