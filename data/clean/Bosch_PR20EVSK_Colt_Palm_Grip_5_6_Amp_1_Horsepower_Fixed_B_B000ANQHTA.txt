Rating: 3.0/5.0 | Date: 05 28, 2007 | Helpful votes: 221
Heading: Good tool
I already own three other "full size" routers so this little router is quite a pleasant surprise to use. It is light weight and very solid; hinge-mortising, edge-triming, and rounding-over are a breeze. I also bought the round subbase so that it can be used with my Porter-Cable templet guide set and it works out quite nicely. So why is only 3-stars? As mentioned by other reviewers, the "Shaft-Locking Button" does not hold and needs to be redesigned. It was the most frustrated thing that I ever dealed with; installing bit is no problem but removing bit was an entirely different story. The button just would not lock tight enough and kept slipping! After sore thumbs and bruised knuckles, I saw in the manual (page 7) suggesting to use a "thin" 10mm wrench as an alternative to the shaft locking button . Out of frustration I took one 10mm wrench from my inexpensive wrench set, grinded it a bit thinner so that it would fit on the flats of the spindle. Now I can easily install or remove bits using two wrenches. BOSCH either needs to redesign the shaft locking button or supply an additional $2.00 wrench!

I just purchased one of the routers. Heading: HUGE disappointment
While Hate is not really a word to use on an inanimate object, it comes pretty close to how I feel about this little machine.I'm a professional contractor and rely on my tools to perform. A small router is a great tool to have as it allows you to work in tight spaces. I have purchased MANY Bosch tools in the past and in general have been happy with the brand. The Bosch Colt, however, has failed in so many ways costing me time and money.Shortcomings:Depth Control: The router base is supposed to clamp to the motor securing the depth. The cam device on this router does not adequately secure the motor. (Yes, I have tightened the nut and cam to no avail). As you move the router across the work piece, I can actually feel the motor slide down into the base resulting in a deepening groove. Not only does this ruin the work, the cutter is easily over-heated causing the work to burn and the bit to grab. This also puts unnecessary stress on the motor.Spindle Lock: The spindle lock is designed to allow you to use one wrench to remove cutters. While a nice idea, the amount of force required to keep the spindle lock from slipping while loosening the collet with the wrench is ridiculous. I have pretty strong hands but I have been tempted to use a clamp to hold that button in when replacing cutters.Fence: The fence is cheaply made and poorly designed. The thumb screw that holds the fence to the base actually hits the fence making it a pain to tighten. The material used to make the fence is thin, stamped steel that bends easily. Adjustment is crude and inaccurate not to mention insecure.What I like about it: The motor and variable speed are great and it is a comfortable router to use.I haven't bought a replacement for the Bosch because I was hoping Bosch would address the design issues. Quite frankly, the competition is even worse (other than the pricey Festool). There is hope, however. Recently a co-worker brought in his Rigid which I was very impressed with. The cutters change out easily and, more importantly, the depth stays true.

--- REVIEW 19 ---
Rating: 4.0/5.0 | Date: 05 28, 2012 | Helpful votes: 3
Heading: Use both wrenches
I've been on the fence over whether or not this thing was worth the price of entry for a few years now, and all the one star reviews were a big factor in that. When I saw that Stewart Macdonald had begun making guitar repair jigs for it I figured it had to be worth a shot and picked one up to help square up some rough edges that resulted from my using a circular saw as a miter saw with varying degrees of success.I immediately found that none of those one-star complaints had any validity to them whatsoever. There is no play in the base at all when it is locked correctly (and it's pretty difficult to lock it incorrectly to boot). The shaft lock button engaged just as well as the one on my big router, the bit was perfectly perpendicular--as verified by a Starrett square--and I didn't really need a manual to get up and going in minutes. I will say that perfect centering is not something you should expect with the included base. It's more of a quick-and-dirty affair than something you'll ever want to do inlay with, but the fact that it doesn't accept templets should be a pretty big clue there.One problem I did experience was the bit dropping out of the collet. This happened twice and I was getting a mite peeved, but then on the third try I used the second wrench rather than the shaft lock button when tightening and had no further problems. This is generally recommended practice with most routers, so I can't fault the Colt for that. The second wrench *is* in the box these days, so don't let those complaints deter you.Didn't think I'd need the variable speed, but it did scorch pine on 6. Backing it off to 5 helped tremendously. It definitely runs warm, but use some common sense and it shouldn't overheat--deep cuts should always be done incrementally no matter how much horsepower you've got to work with.I wouldn't dream of making it my only router, but it beats the pants off of any Dremel I've ever used.
